# Exhibition proposal · Anjana Rao

## What's shown

- Artefact: one A2 archival print, *Deficit Survey, Tank No. 74*
- Identity card (A6), manifesto (A3), label with QR code (105 × 50 mm)
- Nothing else. The press is behind the code; a screen beside a survey sheet would win.

## How it's shown

- Wall. Centre of the print and the manifesto at 147 cm; card at 151 cm, label at 132 cm
- Hanging: unframed, pinned or magnet-hung at the corners, so the paper can be seen to be paper. To agree with the facilitator
- Space needed: 1.9 m of wall, 1.5 m of floor in front, so one person can stand back while another reads
- Light: even, from above. No spot on the black rows; it would lift them to grey

## Reading order

1. The sheet, from across the room. A dark shape with something bitten out of it.
2. The sheet, close. The bites are buildings; the lines are years; the margin is dated.
3. The card and the manifesto. Who would draw this, and why.
4. The code. "You may be living on a tank bed", and a way to find out.

## Sixty seconds

This is Anjana Rao, a surveyor in 2041 who measures tanks that only exist in the records. *(10)*

Bengaluru ran on irrigation tanks. Over a hundred have been built on, most since the seventies, and the city now digs for water instead. *(15)*

The work is made by a system that rules one line across a lost tank for every year of rain, and draws it heavier the more water went missing. After the tank was gone, that is all of it. *(15)*

You're looking at Chinnagara Kere, which is Ejipura now. The white gaps are buildings. Nobody drew them; they're where measuring stopped. *(15)*

The code asks where you live. *(5)*
