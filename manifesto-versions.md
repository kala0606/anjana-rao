# How the manifesto changed

The current text is [manifesto.md](manifesto.md), version 3. Versions 1 and 2 are kept here because the reason for each change is part of the work.

## Version 1 · Day 4

*Written as the kind of first draft the worksheet expects, so the cuts can be seen.*

> I think that maybe the city has kind of forgotten its lakes, which is really sad. I am a surveyor and I believe we should remember the tanks that were here before the roads. My maps show the missing water. I use old maps and rainfall data and I draw the drought of each year on the place where the tank was.

Cut on reading aloud: the hedges, the sentence that explains the identity from outside, and "really sad". The first line worth keeping was the one hidden in it: the city has *agreed* to forget.

## Version 2 · Day 5 (submitted with A1)

> I measure what this city has agreed to forget.
>
> My father kept the boundary stones of a tank that was filled in 2004. I keep his measurements.
>
> Every map of Bengaluru records what the city owns. None records what it has lost. I draw the second map.
>
> I will not make loss beautiful. No aerial views, no blue gradients, no water I have not measured.
>
> My rules: Nothing is drawn that has not been measured. Absence is drawn heavier than presence. Every line carries its date and its source.
>
> A boundary is not a line. It is a promise, and I record where it was broken.
>
> The borewells in the east now go past a thousand feet. I draw that depth too.
>
> You may be living on a tank bed. My map will tell you which one.

## Version 3 · Day 9

Two changes, both forced by the rules in the text itself.

**Struck out: "The borewells in the east now go past a thousand feet."** I went looking for the measurement. The figure appears in newspapers as something residents say ([reference 7](references.md)). What has been measured is the depth to water in Bengaluru East taluk: 17.86 m in March 2015 and 37.76 m in March 2024 ([reference 6](references.md)). It is a smaller, less frightening number, and the first rule says it is the only one allowed on the sheet.

**Added: "The rain did not fail. It fell, and it was not kept."** Version 2 assumed drought. The gauge says otherwise: Bangalore averaged 900 mm a year from 1837 to 1969 and 1,047 mm from 1970 to 2023. See Day 8 in [log.md](log.md).
