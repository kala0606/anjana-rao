# Identity · Anjana Rao, surveyor of tank beds

## A · The condition

**The condition.** The loss of Bengaluru's irrigation tanks to layouts, stadiums, depots and roads, most of it since the 1970s, and the falling water table under the eastern taluks that followed.

**Who lives inside it.** Households on filled tank beds whose ground floors flood. People who buy water by the tanker. Village families whose land was measured by its distance from a bund. The last people who can point to where a sluice stood.

**Three pieces of evidence.**

- 111 lost tanks traced by the Mod Foundation from georeferenced sheets of 1854, 1870, 1897 and 1969 ([ref. 1](references.md))
- The Bangalore rain gauge: 187 complete years, 1837 to 2023, NOAA GHCN-Monthly ([ref. 2](references.md))
- Depth to water in Bengaluru East taluk, 17.86 m in March 2015, 37.76 m in March 2024 ([ref. 6](references.md))

## B · The displacement

**Coordinates moved:** role and time.

**Where it stands.** A government land surveyor in 2041, still sent each season to measure tanks that exist only in the records.

**What can be seen from there.** That the city's maps record what it owns and never what it has lost. A tank goes from the sheet in one edition, without a note. From 2041 the record is all there is, so every weakness in the record becomes visible: who signed it, when, and what was left out.

## C · The facts

**Name, and why.** Anjana Rao. Anjana is the dark line drawn round an eye to sharpen it. Rao is a common surname in Karnataka, the kind that fills a government staff list.

**Origin.** Born in 1999 in a village whose tank was built over in 2004, when she was five. Her father, not a surveyor, kept the tank's boundary stones in the yard because nobody else would. She trained so that she could say exactly where they belonged.

**Practice.** Chain survey, done on foot. Mornings on the ground with a chainman; afternoons in a record room, comparing what she walked with the last sheet that shows water. Her returns are filed and not read.

**Tools and materials.** A chain, a ranging rod, a field book, a 0.3 mm technical pen and black ink. Cotton paper, because the old sheets that survived were on cotton. A plotter or a printer when the record needs a fair copy.

**Lineage.**

- Anuradha Mathur and Dilip da Cunha, *Deccan Traverses*: that surveying did not just record Bangalore's ground, it made it ([ref. 10](references.md))
- Hans Haacke, *Shapolsky et al.* (1971): public records, set out plainly, as an accusation ([ref. 11](references.md))
- Hanne Darboven: numbers written row by row until time becomes a surface you have to stand in front of ([ref. 13](references.md))

## D · Attention

**What it attends to.** Where a measured line has to stop, and what stopped it. The date in the corner of a sheet. Walls that follow a curve for no reason, because the curve was a bund.

**What it refuses.** Aerial images of the city as beautiful pattern. Blue. Gradients. The word "lake", which is what a tank is called after it stops working. Any picture of loss that is pleasant to look at.

**What it would build.** The second map: one sheet for every tank the city no longer draws, to be hung where the people living on its bed can find themselves on it.

## E · Beliefs and rules

**Three beliefs.**

- Only measurement counts as proof. Memory is where you learn what to measure.
- A boundary is a promise that was broken, not a line.
- A year with no record of a tank holding water is a year in which the city has to answer for the rain.

**Three rules.**

- Because only measurement counts as proof, the work must draw nothing that has not been measured.
- Because the city draws what it owns, the work must draw absence heavier than presence.
- Because records are altered by being copied, every line must carry its date and its source.

## F · The distance

**What is shared.** Living in Bengaluru. Walking on tank beds every day without knowing it.

**Where we differ.** She trusts an instrument more than a witness. She has lost a place; the author has only read about one. She has a state employer, and has to make her argument in the form the state accepts.

**What has to be found out.** How a chain is laid across a bed. When each tank was last shown on a sheet. Whether the rain really fell short.
